Benzina

Gasoline. A man, a Harley, a gun in his bag, and a revenge that is not what it seems

Lucio Freni · 2011, 2021 edition · noir novel · Italian · English presentation

Even a dead man can write a book. He can confess, he can take his revenge, but he does not notice the stars watching him, the same stars that are uselessly waiting outside the window tonight. In the half-light, blood looks a lot like ink.

The book

Rome, the fourth of June, midnight. Alfiere Nero, "Black Bishop", is chatting on Facebook with Paola, a woman who lives in a village in Umbria and cannot pick up her son from school tomorrow: the boy will walk home alone, eight or nine hundred metres. Alfiere Nero does not exist. He is the profile behind which Ruggero Landi, forty, tailored suits and a house with a Mercedes in the garage, has spent months winning that woman's trust with copied poems, because "poems love women, women love poetry, women love men who love poems". In the top drawer of the desk there is a cold shape with a smooth barrel. He will act tomorrow.

The novel is that tomorrow. Ruggero shaves to Metallica, pulls the cover off the Harley Davidson his father gave him fourteen years earlier, stops at a cemetery to look for a grave from 1972, and rides out along the Via Flaminia with the gun in his shoulder bag. On the road, as on every journey, the plan gets dirty with life: a petrol-station customer in a pink shirt spitting at the recession, a Romanian girl behind a bar counter, a jealous carabiniere, and Laura, the mechanic's daughter, who keeps Pessoa and Baudelaire on her girlhood dressing table and climbs onto the back of the bike. Then a dog with honey-coloured eyes at the bottom of a ditch, rain under a café's umbrellas, an abbey where time slows down, and a friar who has been waiting for something for forty years.

The revenge comes, but it is not the one the reader has imagined, nor the one Ruggero has told himself; and the envelope handed to a boy in a Dallas Cowboys tracksuit opens an ending in which every line of the book, the rainy robbery of 1972, the father in the blue blazer, the "cunning" country girl, the gasoline running out, closes on the others. Benzina is a sensory noir, alternating third-person narration with italic chapters in which Ruggero speaks in the first person, between the rage of Céline and the geometries of Borges, the two models the author names. The book is published in Italian only; the quotations below are English renderings, with page numbers from the 2021 Italian edition.

The author

Lucio Freni is an independent Italian writer based in Rome. His books span noir, dystopian satire, philosophical grotesque and epistolary fiction, and include Cronache dal Sistema, Il libro del Nulla, Non torna mai nessuno da quaggiù and È tutta colpa di Dio. All titles are published in Italian. Author record on Wikidata.
