Cronache dal Sistema

Chronicles from the System. I: Mr Ciccione and Friar Fifteen. A man erased by an Enter key, and a System that cannot process its own error

Lucio Freni · 2020 · dystopian satire · novella · Italian · English presentation

No operator has ever pressed the "no" option at the edge of the screen, since such an act would have raised his "doubt" score by several points.

The book

It all begins with a fourth-grade temporary clerk at the Registry Office, Deaths Section, who one evening in "Octember" is hot, wants a coffee, and presses Enter. On the other side of the city Mr Ciccione, "Fatso", fifty-five years old, 159 kilos, ham and beer in front of the television, finds the next morning that the eye scanner at the office no longer recognises him after thirty years, that the guard does not know him, that his card is declined at the supermarket, that his mother, over the intercom of her care home, declares she has no children. For the System he is dead, and the System, which counts on the evening news the born, the dead, the born alive and the born dead, has no key to go back.

A supermarket brawl, a cage like the ones at the zoo, a trial in which crimes are paid for in "whole pieces" and crime pays (in the case of a repeat offence, exactly double), two men in balaclavas with no eyeholes: the solution the System finds for the man who does not exist is to give him the identity of a friar who has just died. Ciccione is dropped off in a habit, with a rosary and a suitcase full of cartons of cigarettes, beer, a San Daniele ham and an inflatable doll, in front of a triangular monastery at 2,975 metres, with staircases that go down while going up and doors opening onto the void. He is Friar Fifteen. His brothers, who see a saint where there is a man shouting at the football in his cell, make him their confessor; Friar Two, the abbot, keeps a general's dress uniform under his habit; Friar Twenty-One, once head chef at the restaurant "The Joys of the System", cooks stock-cube soup as penance. And Ciccione, between a roast thrown in the bin and one dream too many, begins to doubt.

The book closes with the postcard the System sends to every citizen on coming of age, with cashier, mechanic, thief, guard, priest or minister written on it, and with a janitor promoted to professor of physics who decides to drive up the mountain to confess. It is the first volume of the Chronicles from the System: dystopian satire in the tradition of Kafka and Orwell, dedicated "to George, Luigi, Franz, Luis-Ferdinand, José, Stefano", but more desperate and funnier, because the protagonist is not a hero; he is an average man who just wants to watch the match. The book is published in Italian only; the quotations below are English renderings, with page numbers from the 2020 Italian edition.

The author

Lucio Freni is an independent Italian writer based in Rome. His books span noir, dystopian satire, philosophical grotesque and epistolary fiction, and include Benzina, Il libro del Nulla, Non torna mai nessuno da quaggiù and È tutta colpa di Dio.
